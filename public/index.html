Flight manual

A walkie-talkie radio for your ship. Every channel is an X Space, another ship full of real people talking right now. Flip through them the way you'd flip through a feed.

The dial browses. The button boards. X plays the sound.

  1. BAND picks what it's about. Rooms on the glass are live; taller ones are busier.
  2. TUNE: turn the knob, swipe the screen, or press the arrow keys .
  3. PUSH TO LISTEN docks X beside the radio. Then press X's Start listening: that second press belongs to X, and the sound plays there. Tell the radio I HEAR IT.
  4. Tap the SPEAKER to see who's aboard: hosts and speakers, linked to their X profiles.
  5. OPEN IN X shows the room on X itself.
  6. SCAN (computer) lines up a new room every few minutes; push to jump.
  7. EN hides rooms X marks as another language.
  8. ◎ RADAR shows every ship on the band as a blip. Nearer the centre means a bigger crowd, and a green halo means voices on the mic. Tap a ship to tune to it.
  9. ＋ at the end of the bands makes your own: a short name and up to two search words, like guitar or Detroit.
  10. BEAM ✦ sends a friend a link that opens their radio on the room you're on, so you can hang out in the same Space.

Why two buttons?

Browsers only let a site make sound after you press something on that site. The radio finds the ship; X opens the hatch. So every room takes two presses: PUSH here, then Start listening in X. The dial browses, the button boards.

Hearing nothing?

  1. Find the X window. It opened beside the radio (or as a new tab).
  2. Press X's Start listening. Logged out, it says Start listening anonymously.
  3. Look for a muted speaker on the X tab, and check your volume.
  4. Rooms go quiet between speakers. Give it 20 seconds.
  5. Signed in to X just now? The room reloads: press Start listening again.
  6. The room may have ended.

Who's aboard

Names come from X, which charges this radio's owner about a cent per person, so they load only when you open the crew, and everyone on the radio shares each scan for two minutes. X shares how many people are listening, not who.

    Listen, talk, lurk

    • Listen: anyone, on a computer or phone, even without an X account (X calls it Start listening anonymously).
    • Talk: needs an X account and the X phone app, because the web can only listen. On a computer, press MIC and scan the code with your phone to carry the room over.
    • Lurk: X has anonymous listening if you'd rather not be seen in the room.
    • Every room plays inside X, so hosts keep their controls. They pick speakers and can mute, remove or block anyone.

    Why "Space"

    The internet is space. Your phone is your ship. Every X Space is another ship, a crowd of people floating together and talking.

    Most of what you find out there is old light: posts and videos that left their source hours or years ago. A Space has no delay, which is why it feels like being around people. This radio picks up their signals.

    Why this exists

    Built in the fall of 2026 by someone who spent the warm months playing guitar in the park, around people. Then winter came and work went remote. Scrolling posts isn't the same as hearing real voices.

    Spaces are the closest thing online to a third place: real people, live, talking about what they care about, and anyone can speak up. This radio makes flipping through them as easy as flipping through a feed. It's company in the background while you work, and a door you can walk through when you feel like talking.
